AN EXACT RANK-TEN DETERMINANT THEOREM, CONDITIONAL ON gamma_9 <= 2
Confidential research continuation, 7 October 2026.

THEOREM. Assume the external Hermite-constant assertion gamma_9<=2.
Every positive-definite even integral lattice L of rank10 and minimum
squared norm at least6 has Gram determinant at least54675. The bound is
attained by the two explicit lattices in rank10_e8_certificate.json.
Thus e_10(6)=54675 under this external assertion.

This statement is not a global classification of equality cases.
Exactly two equality types have been classified in the subclass containing
sqrt(3)E8. The strict global search below does not exclude additional
non-E8-containing equality types at determinant54675.

DEPENDENCIES.
Besides gamma_9<=2, the proof uses the classical Hermite powers
 gamma_r^r =1,4/3,2,4,8,64/3,64,256 for r=1,...,8,
and the uniqueness, among all Euclidean lattices, of the rank-eight
Hermite equality lattice E8 up to similarity. Mordell's inequality is
proved below. The E8-containing rank-ten lemma is proved from the exact
finite E8/3E8 table and the elementary binary Hermite bound. The remaining
input is a complete finite exact search with saved coverage and maps.
NO previous rank-nine determinant theorem, uniqueness theorem, spectral
gap theorem, or their older certificates are used in this rank-ten proof.
The saved low-rank9 search independently covers the formerly gap-dependent
interval. The original rank-nine K9 matrix is used only to display convenient
E8 and rank-ten witnesses, whose properties are directly checked anew.

1. Normalizations and parity.
For a lattice L, det(L) is the determinant of its Gram matrix, i.e. the
square of its covolume. min(L) is the minimum nonzero squared norm.
An even integral Gram matrix is integral, symmetric, positive definite
and has even diagonal entries. In odd rank its determinant is even. In
rank2m it is congruent to0 or(-1)^m modulo4. These elementary determinant
congruences can be proved by the determinant expansion, or by integral
alternating reduction modulo4. In particular a rank10 counterexample has
 D=det(L)<=54672,
because54673 and54674 are congruent to1 and2 modulo4, respectively.

2. Saturated shortest-dual flags.
Given L_r, choose a shortest nonzero vector y_r of L_r*. It is primitive,
since divisibility by an integer>1 would give a shorter nonzero dual
vector. Thus x-><x,y_r> maps L_r onto Z. Its kernel
 L_(r-1)=L_r intersect y_r^perp
is primitive, has rankr-1, and is even integral with minimum at least6.
Choosing a lift of1 and comparing covolumes gives
 d_(r-1)=d_r ||y_r||^2=d_r min(L_r*).
Iterating produces a saturated flag through all ranks. The Hermite bound
for the dual gives
 d_(r-1)<=gamma_r*d_r^((r-1)/r).
The primal minimum gives d_r>=6^r/gamma_r^r.

Mordell's inequality follows directly. Normalize det(M)=1 in rankn.
Its shortest-dual kernel J satisfies
 min(M)<=min(J)<=gamma_(n-1)*det(J)^(1/(n-1))
 <=gamma_(n-1)*gamma_n^(1/(n-1)).
Taking the supremum over M and rearranging yields
 gamma_n<=gamma_(n-1)^((n-1)/(n-2)).
Consequently gamma10<=2^(9/8) under gamma9<=2.

4. Excluding every E8-containing counterexample.
For J=sqrt(3)E8, enumeration through unscaled squared norm8 gives all
6561 classes of E8/3E8, with minimum distribution
 (0,1),(2,240),(4,2160),(6,2240),(8,1920).
Thus every class of J*/J has minimum<=8/3. Any integral overlattice of J
of the same rank which properly contains J would therefore contain a
nonzero vector of norm<=8/3; so J is automatically primitive in any
ambient integral lattice of minimum>=6.

If a rank-ten lattice L contains J, project onto its orthogonal binary
quotient P. Every nonzero z in P has a lift whose parallel part is a
minimum representative of a class in J*/J. Therefore
 ||z||^2>=6-8/3=10/3.
Binary Gauss reduction gives det(P)>=(3/4)min(P)^2. Thus
 det(L)=6561 det(P)>=(3/4)*6561*(10/3)^2=54675.
This excludes such L from the strict counterexample search.

If a shortest-dual rank-eight flag member has determinant6561, equality
in the rank-eight Hermite bound implies that it is sqrt(3)E8. That would
be contained in L, just excluded. Rank-eight determinant congruence then
raises its lower endpoint to6564. No other rank-eight lattice is removed
on the basis of an E8 test. The final search windows are therefore:

5. Complete exact one-vector extension.
Suppose a primitive rank-r parent J has integral Gram G, determinant d,
adjugate A, minimum>=6, and is even. Every primitive one-rank extension
can be represented by
 H=[[G,p],[p^T,n]], p in Z^r, n an even integer.
Put s=n-p^T G^{-1}p>0 and D=det(H)=ds. Replacing the last generator by
an element of J permits p to be chosen from a closest representative of
a class in J*/J. Its fingerprint is A p modulo d. This finite group has
d classes. Complete dual-vector enumeration until all d fingerprints
appear gives the exact minimum of every class, not a heuristic cover.
Write M_a=d*mu_J(a), an integer. The extension has minimum>=6 iff
 m^2 D + M_(ma) >=6d
for all positive integers m with m^2D<6d. The parent already checks m=0;
negative m give the same minima. Nonzero zero-class layers are included.
The diagonal interval, determinant congruence and these finitely many
layer inequalities are checked exactly.

For a shortest-dual flag extension, the normal must itself be shortest
in H*. This can be checked by direct inversion and exact enumeration,
as in the original kernel. The faster equivalent test is also exact:
for q!=0 in Z^r, minimize the block-inverse dual norm over m in Z,
 q^T G^{-1}q+(m-p^T G^{-1}q)^2/s.
The normal has squared norm d/D. Consequently it is shortest iff
 D(q^T A q)+dist(p^T A q,d Z)^2>=d^2
for every q with D(q^T A q)<d^2. One parent-dual list through d/D_low
contains every q needed for any D>=D_low. This test uses only integers.
The complete derivation and implementation contracts are in
fast_extension_proof.txt and fast_extend.py. The old and new kernels
were compared on exact ordered child lists, including large branches;
additional independent low-rank tests and direct primal/dual checks passed.

6. Lifting exclusions.
Let u be ANY nonzero vector of J*. It lifts to H* with added normal
coefficient of absolute value<=1/2 after choosing a nearest integer.
If the new perpendicular dual vector is shortest, then
 1/s <= ||u||^2+1/(4s),
so s>=3/(4||u||^2). Taking a shortest u is strongest, but is not necessary
for validity. In the retained basis the last dual coordinate vector has
squared norm d_prev/d. Thus a necessary condition for a further child of
determinant at most U is
 3d^2 <= 4 d_prev U.
This is the exact integer inequality used for every recorded lifting
prune. It remains valid even if one does not assume that this chosen
parent-dual vector is shortest. In addition, the certificate checks the
shortest-dual property of retained flag representatives independently.

7. Isometry reductions preserve complete coverage.
Every raw candidate is either excluded by the preceding necessary
lifting inequality or is mapped to a retained representative by an
explicit unimodular integer matrix T satisfying
 T^T G_rep T=G_raw.
The representative is selected from actual raw matrices; its flag basis
is not replaced by an unverified reduced basis. Each map is checked
exactly. Any continuation of an omitted isometric parent transports to
a continuation of its representative, preserving the normal-shortest
property and all intrinsic lattice conditions. The earlier flag itself
need not be fixed by the isometry. Signatures are used only to separate
potential isometry comparisons; no identification is accepted without
an exact checked map. Retaining extra representatives would merely
increase the search, not invalidate its completeness.

BOUNDARIES.
The theorem depends on the external gamma9<=2 assertion. All consequences
must retain that dependency unless it is adopted as an established cited
theorem. No claim is made to classify all rank-ten minimizers, all rank48
primitive embeddings, or all antipode clusters. For bare dimension38
cross-section packings at the inherited squared minimum6, the theorem
excludes improving the determinant below54675. It does not establish
unrestricted optimality of sphere packing in dimension38.
