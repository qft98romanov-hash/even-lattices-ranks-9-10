## Why the 561 classes cover every optimal supersection

Let J be the printed leading copy of sqrt(3)E8, and x the ninth generator of the fixed K9. Primitivity of the printed K9 in P48p implies it is primitive in any intermediate rank-ten section, and J remains primitive because it is generated by eight members of a basis of K9.

For any rank-ten lattice M containing this K9 with determinant 54675, the quotient projected perpendicular to J is a binary lattice of determinant `54675/6561=25/3`. The certified J-dual maximum coset minimum 8/3 and minimum-six condition imply every nonzero projected vector has squared norm at least 10/3: reduce its J component to a shortest representative and use the norm-six lower bound. The binary Hermite inequality then forces equality and the triangular binary Gram matrix `(5/3)[[2,1],[1,2]]`.

The projection of the fixed ninth generator x already has squared norm 10/3, so it is a shortest vector of this triangular quotient and can serve as the first element of an acute triangular basis. Consequently a second generator z can be chosen with projected squared norm 10/3 and inner product 5/3 with the projection of x. Both z and x-z have projected squared norm 10/3. Their J-dual classes b and a-b must therefore both have minimum 8/3, since each whole vector can be reduced in its J coset and must retain squared norm at least 6.

Write the J Gram matrix as 3C, with C the E8 Gram matrix, and write p for the first eight pairings of x. Put `a=C^{-1}p modulo 3`. A deep class b is represented by an integer vector v with `v^T C v=8`. For its shortest J-dual vector, the first eight pairings of z are `C v`, and the ninth pairing is

`(p^T v+5)/3`.

This is the target used by the preparation code. Adjusting z by an element of J changes its representative to the particular shortest v chosen by that code, without changing the generated supersection. Thus choosing one shortest representative for each deep class loses no section. Integrality of every resulting target is explicitly checked.
