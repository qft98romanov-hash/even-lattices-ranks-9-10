## Most relevant current source

Mathieu Dutour Sikirić and Wessel van Woerden, *The lattice packing problem in dimension 9 by Voronoi's algorithm*, arXiv:2508.20719, version 2, 7 February 2026.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20719
- https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20719v2
- Retrieval references: `turn1search0`, `turn2view0`, `turn8view0`, `turn9view0`.

They enumerate 2,237,251,040 perfect similarity classes in dimension 9 and obtain gamma_9=2. They classify possible kissing numbers and provide public data (about 150 GB) and access utilities. Section 5.2 identifies the unique perfect form Q129 with 258 minimal vectors and cites Watson's earlier classification of this high-kissing case; Table 3 independently lists one such perfect class. The root agent computed an exact perfection certificate for our K9: 129 pairs of minimal vectors and 45 linearly independent outer products, with a 45-by-45 minor of determinant 3. Consequently our K9 belongs to this known similarity class, without requiring the full classification of nonperfect cases. This does not establish that every minimizer of our discrete integral determinant problem has 258 minimal vectors. A constrained integral optimum need not automatically be a perfect real form.

The potential new claim must concern the global even-integral minimum-six bound and/or equality classification, not the existence of a previously unknown rank-nine lattice or the value of gamma_9.

## An exact rank-ten bound derived here

Assuming our established e9(6)=21870 and importing gamma_9=2, Mordell's inequality gives gamma_10 <= 2^(9/8). For the shortest-dual primitive rank-nine section J of any eligible rank-ten lattice L of Gram determinant D,

    21870 <= det(J) <= gamma_10 D^(9/10).

Hence

    D >= (21870 / 2^(9/8))^(10/9) = 27910.4416533049368... .

The numerical threshold was checked by the exact integer comparison

    27910^72 * 2^90 < 21870^80 < 27911^72 * 2^90.

The next integer, 27911, has the allowed rank-ten even-lattice determinant congruence (3 modulo 4). Therefore e10(6) >= 27911. This is only a lower bound, not a solution of e10. The plain Hermite bound alone gives 24828.

## Classical constructions and scope checks

J. H. Conway and N. J. A. Sloane, *Complex and Integral Laminated Lattices*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 280 (1983), 463–490; DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-1983-0716832-0.

- Author-hosted primary PDF: https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me106.pdf
- Retrieval references: `turn6view2`, `turn7view1`, `turn7view2`.

Table V concerns minima 2, 3 and 4. Section 3.10 notes the scaled-root-lattice optima for even prescribed minimum through rank eight. This source does not itself supply a global rank-nine/rank-ten minimum-six classification in the portions inspected. Integral lamination fixes a recursive construction and is not interchangeable with unrestricted determinant optimization.

Jacques Martinet and Christian Batut's lattice catalogue:

- https://jamartin.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Lattices/index.html
- https://jamartin.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Lattices/Min6.gp
- https://jamartin.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Lattices/Tables_3.pdf
- Retrieval references: `turn3view0`, `turn4view1`, `turn5view0`, `turn5view1`, `turn6view1`.

The minimum-six table explicitly collects examples and sections/extensions. It is not a complete classification. Its rank-ten Kq10 example has determinant 62208; its rank-nine Kq9 example has determinant 41472, so these particular examples do not improve our targets. The guide distinguishes ordinary, strong, and integral laminations.

Gabriele Nebe and Boris Venkov, *The strongly perfect lattices of dimension 10*, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 12 (2000), 503–518.

- https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2000__12_2_503_0.pdf
- Retrieval references: `turn18search0`, `turn20view1`.

Theorem 3.1 classifies exactly two similarity classes of strongly perfect rank-ten lattices, represented by K'_10 and its dual. Strong perfection means that the minimal vectors form a spherical 4-design. It is an extra assumption absent from the problem here, so this theorem does not settle the unrestricted minimum determinant.

W. Plesken and M. Pohst, *Constructing Integral Lattices with Prescribed Minimum. II*, Math. Comp. 60 (1993), 817–825.

- https://www.jstor.org/stable/2153121
- DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-1993-1176715-1
- Retrieval reference: `turn17search0` (publisher archive abstract).

The stated subject is integral laminated lattices of minimum 4 generated by minimum vectors, not arbitrary rank-ten minimum-six lattices.

## Earlier high-kissing identification

Wessel P. J. van Woerden, *Perfect Quadratic Forms: an Upper Bound and Challenges in Enumeration*, MSc thesis, Leiden, 2018, section 10.2–10.3.

- https://wesselvanwoerden.com/publication/van-2018-perfect/msc-scriptie-wpj-van-woerden.pdf
- Retrieval references: `turn11view0`, `turn14view0`.

The thesis describes Q129 as layers of E8 with 240 minimum vectors in the parent and 18 further minimum vectors. Its minimal-vector projectors have ranks 36 and 9 in complementary spans, proving perfection. This provides a specific historical structural comparison for our 258-vector example.

G. L. Watson, *The number of minimum points of a positive quadratic form*, Dissertationes Mathematicae 84 (1971), 1–46.

- https://eudml.org/doc/268650
- The 2026 paper cites and uses this work. The original PDF could not be fetched (403), so its precise hypotheses were not independently checked from the original. Do not claim to have read it fully.

## Status of novelty and external checking

The targeted search found closely related classifications and a specific known similarity class for the attaining rank-nine example. It did not locate a source that, in the inspected statements, proves our exact unrestricted even-integral determinant claim. Failure to locate such a source is not a proof of novelty. A publication should cite the works above, phrase the claimed contribution precisely, and obtain an actual independent expert review. No external correctness verification or referee verdict has been obtained in this work session.
