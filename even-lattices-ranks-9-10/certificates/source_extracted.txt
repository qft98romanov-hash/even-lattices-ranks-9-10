1      Introduction
A sphere packing in Rn is a collection of congruent balls with disjoint interiors, and its density is the fraction
of space the balls cover. Following Conway and Sloane [9], we measure packings by their center density

in dimensions 1, 2, 3 [13], 8 [25] and 24 [6]; in every other dimension the state of the art is a record set by
an explicit construction, and we refer to the table maintained by Cohn [5].
    Around dimension 48 the densest packings known come from the extremal even unimodular lattices of
that dimension and their cross-sections. In Laminated lattices, Conway and Sloane [7, Corollary 8] exhibited
cross-sections of P48p that set the records in dimensions 39–43, and those records stood for four decades;
the record in dimension 38 was held by a lattice of another family, D38 of center density 8 [9, p. xxxvii].
The antipode construction of Conway and Sloane [8], which places several translates of a cross-section over
a cluster in its dual, improved dimensions 44–47; Chen et al. [3] applied it to deliberately suboptimal cross-
sections and improved dimensions 19, 20, 21, 23, 44, 45 and 47.
    This paper improves all six dimensions from 38 to 43 (Table 1), and, more to the point, derives them
from a single mechanism rather than six separate tricks.

2     Preliminaries
2.1    Cross-sections
Let Λ be an n-dimensional lattice of minimal squared norm µ and let Rn = U ⊕ V be an orthogonal
decomposition with dim U = k, dim V = l = n − k. Write

                                K = Λ ∩ U,       L = Λ ∩ V,     M = πU (Λ),

where πU , πV are the orthogonal projections, and assume K has rank k. A sublattice K ⊆ Λ is saturated
(or primitive) when Λ/K is torsion-free; Λ ∩ U is always saturated, and conversely a saturated sublattice is
the intersection of Λ with its own span. For Λ unimodular, discriminant gluing identifies the discriminant
groups of K and L anti-isometrically [17, Prop. 1.9.8], [7, Thm. 4], so that

                                         det L = det K = 1/ det M.                                          (2)

We write K ∗ and L∗ for the duals of K in U and of L in V . The next fact is used throughout; it needs L
saturated, which is automatic, and not K saturated.
Lemma 3. If Λ is unimodular and L = Λ ∩ V , then πV (Λ) = L∗ . Likewise πU (Λ) = K ∗ when K = Λ ∩ U .
Proof. For x ∈ Λ and ℓ ∈ L we have ⟨πV (x), ℓ⟩ = ⟨x, ℓ⟩ ∈ Z, so πV (Λ) ⊆ L∗ . Conversely let y ∈ L∗ . Since
Λ/L is torsion-free it is free, so L is a direct summand of Λ and the homomorphism ℓ 7→ ⟨y, ℓ⟩ extends
to a homomorphism Λ → Z. As Λ is unimodular, that homomorphism is ⟨x, ·⟩ for some x ∈ Λ, and then
πV (x) − y is orthogonal to L and lies in V = span(L), hence vanishes.

2.3    The ceiling of a bare cross-section
A cross-section of Λ is a lattice of minimum at least µ = 6, and that alone limits how dense its complement
can be. The argument is that of Conway and Sloane, who observed that the densest k-dimensional and
(48 − k)-dimensional sections of a unimodular lattice have the same determinant [7, Corollary 5]; with the
classification of the densest lattices in dimensions at most 8 [7, Theorem 2] this bounds one in terms of the
other. They drew the conclusion in dimensions 43 and above, over even unimodular ambients [7, Corollary 8];
we need it below dimension 43 as well, where their own sections do not attain it (Corollary 11), and over
every unimodular ambient.

48 also has minimum at most 6 by the shadow bound of Rains and Sloane [24], so the ceiling holds over
every unimodular ambient of rank 48.

E7 and E8 , unique up to similarity [17, Ch. 6].1 Those are the ranks at which this paper uses published
optimality results. Section 4 evaluates the bound rank by rank.

3.2    Extension and the glue radius
We now push past rank 8. The determinant of a one-vector extension is governed by a projection, and
integrality restricts that projection to a dual-lattice coset.
Proposition 12. Let K = Λ ∩ U be a saturated cross-section of rank k of a unimodular lattice Λ, and
L = Λ ∩ V with V = U ⊥ .
  1. Every saturated cross-section of rank k + 1 containing K is K + Zv for some v ∈ Λ.
  2. det(K + Zv) = det K · |πV (v)|2 .


                                                       6
  3. The smallest determinant of a rank-(k +1) cross-section containing K is det K ·µ(L∗ ), where µ denotes
     the minimum.
Proof. (1) If W ⊇ K is a saturated section of rank k + 1 then W/K is torsion-free of rank 1, hence infinite
cyclic; any preimage v of a generator gives W = K + Zv. (2) In a basis of K followed by v, the Gram matrix
of K + Zv has the Gram matrix of K as its leading block and |v|2 in the corner; its Schur complement is
|v|2 − |πU (v)|2 = |πV (v)|2 . (3) By Lemma 3, πV maps Λ onto L∗ , so the values |πV (v)|2 for v ∈ Λ with
πV (v) ̸= 0 are exactly the nonzero squared norms of L∗ , and the smallest is µ(L∗ ). If the resulting K + Zv
is not saturated, its saturation is a section of the same rank and strictly smaller determinant containing K,
so the minimum is attained by a saturated section.

γn−1 the minimum of the projection of Λn orthogonal to Λn−1 [7, §II]. They grow a lattice; we grow a section
inside a fixed ambient, where Lemma 3 identifies the projection as L∗ .

6    Search and verification
Everything that decides a claim in this paper is computed in exact integer or rational arithmetic; floating
point and lattice reduction appear only inside search heuristics.
    The seed of Section 3.1 needs no search: σ is written down in closed form on the coordinate frame of the
ternary code, its fixed lattice is an integer kernel computation, and the 480 minimal vectors of that fixed
lattice split into two orthogonal root systems of type E8 , from which a basis with Gram matrix 3C(E8 ) is
read off.
    The chain of Section 3 was found by sweeping the complete list of 52,416,000 minimal vectors of the
ambient once, classifying each by its projection onto the span of the current section (Remark 3), and ranking
extensions by the exact Schur complement of Proposition 12(2). The optimality of each step is not a search
result: ρ2glue (K) is computed by enumerating the det K classes of K ∗ /K (2187, 4374, 6561, 21870 and 54675
classes respectively) and taking, for each class, the minimum norm of its representatives, a finite computation
of rank at most 10. As a check, the
                                 √ dual shortest-vector problem of Proposition 12(3) was also solved directly
in the complement for the step 3E8 → K9 , with the enumeration running to completion, and returned the
same value µ(L∗ ) = 10/3.
    The dimension-43 section of Section 4 was found by simulated annealing over 5-tuples of ambient lattice
vectors: a move replaces or perturbs one vector of the tuple, each tuple is completed to the saturated section
its span meets, and the section is scored by an exact lower bound for the best antipode density over its
dual; the best candidates are then re-scored exhaustively, enumerating all short dual points and running a
maximum-clique search over admissible pairs for each realizable diameter. Independent runs over P48n and
over P48m converged to isometric sections.


                                                          12
    Each packing is recorded as a machine-checkable certificate that names the ambient lattice, the section
basis in ambient coordinates and the cluster, and a verifier rebuilds the ambient from its published con-
struction and re-proves membership, saturation, det L = det K, the cluster distances, the minimum, and the
density. The code, the verifier and certificates for every packing in this paper, in each of the ambients where
it exists, are available at https://github.com/wcgbg/sphere-packing.


7     Open problems
Table 4 localizes what is left for the bare cross-section route and separates unconditional ceilings from
current-record benchmarks. In dimensions 40, 41 and 42 the bare route is closed: the sections of Corollary 11
are optimal among bare cross-section packings. This does not rule out antipode clusters over these or other
sections. In dimension 43 the bare route is also closed, while the cluster route is essential for our improvement
and may admit further gains.
    The interesting bare cases are dimensions 39 and 38. In dimension 39, any rank-9 section with determinant
below 21870 would improve our present packing. The current published rank-9 lattice record corresponds
to the benchmark 19683: under the published-literature convention used here, a determinant below that
is not excluded by an optimality theorem, but would simultaneously produce a new 9-dimensional lattice
record. Thus [19683, 21870) is the portion of the search interval that could improve dimension 39 without
necessarily improving that lower-dimensional record. If the recent dimension-9 preprint cited above is correct,
then determinants below 19683 are excluded and this interval is the full rigorous search window. Likewise,
for dimension 38 one seeks determinant below 54675. The current rank-10 lattice record gives the exact
benchmark 311 /4 = 44286.75; because section determinants are integral, det K ≤ 44286 would also improve
the rank-10 lattice record, while 44287 ≤ det K < 54675 could improve dimension 38 without doing so.
    Any such section inside an even ambient lattice must itself be an even integral lattice of minimum at
least 6, which is apreal constraint: at the rank-9 benchmark 19683 = 39 the natural candidate, the rescaled
laminated lattice 3/2 Λ9 , is not integral, so a rank-9 section of that determinant would have to be some
other lattice. We do not know which even integral lattices of minimum 6 occur in ranks 9 and 10, and
determining their smallest determinant would sharpen these searches. The current lattice records alone do
not provide rigorous determinant floors in these ranks; the recent dimension-9 preprint, if correct, supplies
the rank-9 floor. We leave the corresponding integral-section questions open.
